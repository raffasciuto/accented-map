DATASET-WIDE SOURCES
----------------------------------------------------------------------
Web-share percentages: Common Crawl CC-MAIN-2026-30
Cross-referenced with: W3Techs, Aug 2026
Language lists: Wikipedia, Languages of <Country> (e.g. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Languages_of_<Country>)

SCHEMA / METHODOLOGY
----------------------------------------------------------------------
langs: [name, webPct|null, primary] - primary is 1 if this language is genuinely the first/home language of a real population in this country today (whether the country's ethnic majority, or a real native minority/settler-descended community), 0 if it is an administrative/elite lingua franca layered over a population whose real everyday language is something else (English in Nigeria, Urdu in Pakistan, French in Senegal). A language only counts toward a country's AI-representation score if primary=1. Locally-formed creoles (Nigerian Pidgin, Tok Pisin, Papiamento) count as primary. Colonial-origin languages CAN be primary when they became a population's genuine native tongue through settlement (English in USA/UK/Australia/Canada; Spanish in Argentina/Mexico/Peru/Colombia; Portuguese in Brazil) - the test is 'is this really someone's mother tongue here', not the language's origin. Migrant/diaspora languages (brought by recent immigrant communities, not the country's own people) are omitted entirely, not merely flagged 0.

total: Total distinct languages the source article documents for that country. Values marked approximate in _notes were not stated outright by the article.

additional: total minus the number of languages named here - rendered as 'and N further documented languages, none tracked in web-crawl data'.

Language representation dataset for the About-page globe. Language lists were extracted from each country's English Wikipedia 'Languages of <Country>' article (fetched 2026-08-02/03). Web-share percentages come from Common Crawl language statistics, crawl CC-MAIN-2026-30, cross-referenced with W3Techs (Aug 2026). A null percentage means the language does not appear in Common Crawl's language table at all - it is not tracked in web-crawl data. That absence is itself the finding, not missing work.

PER-COUNTRY METHODOLOGY NOTES / CAVEATS
----------------------------------------------------------------------
United States (USA): No de jure federal official language. Executive Order 14224 (2025) declared English official at federal level by executive act. Spanish kept at primary:0 despite its scale (Puerto Rico, Southwest) - it is real and long-standing but not the majority home language nationally; unlike Nigeria/India's English, this is a judgment call about scale, not administrative-overlay status.

Mexico (MEX): Spanish is de facto national with no constitutional designation; the 68 Indigenous languages are statutorily national languages under the LGDLPI.

Mali (MLI): Since the 2023 constitution the 13 national languages are the official ones; French was demoted to a working language, kept here at primary:0 as a real administrative holdover, not removed.

Niger (NER): Hausa became the sole official language in 2025, replacing French, which is now only a working language (primary:0, kept not removed).

Nigeria (NGA): Wikipedia's claimed French co-official status was never constitutionally implemented and has been dropped entirely - not official, not native, not migrant, just not real.

Pakistan (PAK): Urdu is marked primary:0: it is a lingua franca native to north India, not indigenous to any Pakistani province - the same treatment given to Afrikaans-style administrative overlays elsewhere. Reasonable reviewers could contest this.

Turkey (TUR): Turkish is treated as primary, though Greek, Armenian and Syriac are older languages of Anatolia. A reviewer could reasonably contest this classification.

United Kingdom (GBR): Total of 300 reflects the article's census 'language categories', which include immigrant-community languages, not a purely linguistic count.

Netherlands (NLD): Papiamento reclassified to primary:1 - it is the native creole of the Caribbean Netherlands (Bonaire), not a migrant language, despite the earlier dataset flagging it otherwise.

South Africa (ZAF): English and Afrikaans both marked primary:1 - each is the genuine native home language of a real (non-majority) South African population descended from settlers, not merely an elite administrative layer over the Black-majority population the way Nigeria's English is. This is a deliberate departure from the Nigeria/Kenya/India treatment of English.

Singapore (SGP): All four official languages (English, Mandarin, Malay, Tamil) marked primary:1 - each is a real, substantial home-language community in Singapore (58.1% / 26.6% / 7.0% / 2.2% of homes respectively, 2025), unlike Nigeria-style English with near-zero native speakers.

Kazakhstan (KAZ): Russian marked primary:1 alongside Kazakh - Kazakhstan retains a large, genuinely native (not merely administrative) ethnic-Russian minority.

Finland (FIN): Swedish marked primary:1 alongside Finnish - Finland-Swedes (~5%) are a genuine multi-generational native minority, not an administrative overlay.

Switzerland (CHE): Romansh marked primary:1 despite being a minority even within its home canton - it is federally official and genuinely native, unlike Ireland's Irish or New Zealand's Maori (see IRL/NZL notes).

Ireland (IRL): Irish marked primary:0 - official-in-law as the first official language, but daily-spoken by a small minority outside the Gaeltacht; treated the same as New Zealand's Maori for consistency, despite Irish being indigenous to the territory (unlike Nigeria-style English).

New Zealand (NZL): Maori marked primary:0 - official in law but only ~4% of the population speaks it, even among Maori only ~18%. English, though only de facto official, is the genuine ~95% majority native language.

Afghanistan (AFG): Dari and Pashto both marked primary:1 - Afghanistan has no single clean linguistic majority; Dari functions as the larger lingua franca+L1 combined, Pashto as the largest single ethnic group's language. Treated as a genuine dual-primary case, like Belgium or Bolivia.

Bolivia (BOL): Guarani kept at primary:0 despite official status - only ~33,000 speakers, too small relative to Bolivia's other co-official majority-native languages (Quechua, Aymara) to count toward the score.

Georgia (GEO): Abkhaz and Russian both kept at primary:0 - Abkhaz's official status applies only to the disputed breakaway region, not Georgian-controlled territory; Russian is a Soviet-era administrative lingua franca without a comparably large genuine native Georgian-Russian population.
